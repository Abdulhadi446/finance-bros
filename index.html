What's modeled: monthly contributions until the selected stop date, annual contribution raises, smooth or illustrative year-to-year growth ranges, dividends paid monthly with optional reinvestment, dividend tax, annual fees, inflation, blended asset weights, and optional monthly withdrawals.

What isn't: the volatility range is a deterministic ±25% stress illustration, not a forecast or probability model. Asset correlations, market timing, capital-gains tax on selling, transaction costs, sequence-of-returns risk, and dividend cuts are not modeled. This remains a planning sketch, not investment advice.

Quick guide & FAQs

How do I run a monthly calculation?

Select Months under Calculate by. The horizon, chart, stop date, and dividend table will all use monthly periods.

What does “stop contributions” do?

It stops new deposits after the selected month. Existing money continues to grow, receive dividends, and follow withdrawals for the rest of the horizon.

What is the difference between dividend cash and reinvested?

Dividend, cash leaves after-tax dividends outside the invested balance. Dividend, reinvested adds after-tax dividends back into the balance.

How should I use Portfolio blend?

Choose the blend mode, set each contribution share, and make the total equal 100%. Each preset keeps its own growth, yield, fee, and tax assumptions.

What does the volatility range mean?

It shows illustrative low and high paths using 75% and 125% of the average monthly growth. It is a stress range, not a prediction or probability estimate.

Are the preset returns guaranteed?

No. Presets are editable planning assumptions based on broad historical-style examples. They do not forecast any security or account.

How are taxes and inflation handled?

Dividend tax reduces dividend cash and reinvestment. The Today’s $ view discounts nominal values using the annual inflation assumption.

What should I check before relying on a result?

Try lower and higher growth, change the contribution stop date, compare cash versus reinvested dividends, and test a withdrawal amount. Treat the output as a scenario sketch.
